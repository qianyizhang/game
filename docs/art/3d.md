## Explore and export

Drag to orbit; scroll or pinch to zoom. Named camera buttons also work by keyboard. **Play/Pause animation**, speed selection and a scrubbable timeline inspect the six-second loops. Reduced-motion preferences disable initial playback. Layer separation is available for the procedural Nightjar, Catalyst and Phoenix studies and pauses motion while active. Published native deliveries remain assembled. Spiral, Vajra, Hydra, Prowler, Wolf, Matriarch, Thornstag, Moonmoth, Bogtoad, Crocolisk, Scavenger, Guardian, Tortoise, Stormroc, Stray, Pack Caller, Cub, Amalgam, Imp, Matron, Juggler, Watcher, Herald, Patron and Squire retain their assembled form; the gallery disables layer separation for these studies. Slow turntable, wireframe and three lighting presets remain available. Returning restores keyboard focus to the gallery entry.

**Download 3D model** returns the selected reviewed native release’s exact GLB bytes, or exports the retained procedural study on demand. The assembled binary glTF (`.glb`) contains named meshes, embedded material dependencies and authored animation. It excludes the plinth, lighting, current camera and inspection transforms. The following construction notes describe the studies and their retained procedural implementations; current native authoring authority is identified below. Hydra includes a 28-bone skin rig: the coil stays anchored while three neck chains deform the flesh and fitted armor; heads and jaws move at their own pivots. Prowler and Wolf each use a ten-bone rig with anchored paws, a blended neck/head transition and a separate tail chain. Matriarch uses a three-bone anchor/neck/head rig; the broad body, paws and claws stay planted. Thornstag also uses three bones: three hooves stay on the plinth, a fourth stays raised, and the antler crown follows the head; ears and a short tail move at fitted pivots. Moonmoth has four skinned wings with fitted hinges and delayed hindwing-tail flex; its body and six twig grips stay fixed. Bogtoad uses an anchor/head/throat rig: the crouch and toes stay fixed while a localized throat region breathes and the head moves slightly. Crocolisk uses six bones for anchored body, head, localized breathing and three distal-tail frames; fitted armor shares that rig, while the jaw and eyes move at their own pivots. Scavenger uses five bones for anchored paws, a blended neck/head response and two tail frames; the fine mane fringe shares that rig, while ears and eyes move at fitted pivots. Guardian also uses five bones: the hind feet and raised hands stay fixed while neck/head and tail frames move; the ears, eyes and whisker fans follow fitted pivots. Tortoise uses three bones for anchored body and blended neck/head motion; the shell, claws, forelimb plates and plants stay fixed. Stormroc uses three bones for the pelvis-to-head surface and its fitted contours, with separate shoulder, wrist, tail and eye pivots; both grips remain fixed on their ledge. Stray uses five bones for anchored support and a blended neck/head response with two tail frames; the raised paw stays folded, and the ears and eyes follow fitted pivots. Pack Caller uses five bones for body, neck/head and tail, with the fitted collar sharing the rig; its jaw articulates at a skull pivot, and the four soles and pendant remain fixed. Cub uses three bones for its anchored body and blended neck/head movement; its four paws and twenty claws remain fixed. Amalgam uses nine distinct bones across a continuous body-to-wrist surface, fitted chest plate, wing fingers and eight thin membrane panels; the four feet stay planted while the neck, head, shoulders, wrists and tail respond. Imp uses seven distinct bones for its anchored crouch, blended neck/head and wing shoulders/wrists; wing membranes share the body’s attachment weights, and both hands and feet remain fixed. Matron uses five distinct bones across two skins: a continuous chest/neck/head and crown-to-tip hair mass; her robe, hands, boots and pendant stay fixed while the horns and crown follow the head. Juggler uses three bones for its anchored stance and blended neck/head response; raised hands and feet stay fixed while two closed spirit-flame assemblies drift independently. Patron uses three bones for the neck and face, with four fitted lids; hands, short coat, trousers and boots stay fixed. Herald uses three bones across the continuous neck and face, with four fitted skin lids; both boots, hands, robe and forked staff stay fixed. Watcher uses three bones across the body and fitted jerkin; its feet, hands and hip wrap stay fixed, with a scanning eye and fitted upper/lower eyelid pivots. Nightjar and Phoenix also use skinned continuous body/neck surfaces, with facial parts following the head frame and articulated feather groups moving around fitted pivots. Their lower bodies and grips stay anchored. The remaining studies animate node transforms around authored pivots. These are authored loops, not biological simulations. Glass uses glTF transmission and alpha blending; the liquid uses a translucent layer so its contents remain visible in the real-time renderer. These are appearance approximations, not a simulation of nested optical media. Appearance depends on the importing viewer.

**Save animation loop** records six seconds of actual canvas frames as WebM, from the current camera and lighting. It records the authored animation at 1× speed with the turntable stationary, including when preview playback is paused. Keep the gallery visible during recording; changing tabs or leaving the gallery cancels it. Controls lock during capture. WebM recording requires browser MediaRecorder/canvas capture support; unsupported browsers can still export animated GLBs. **Save image** captures the current rendered view as PNG.

## Verification

Run `npm run check` and `npm run test:browser -- tests/browser/art3d.spec.ts tests/browser/shell.spec.ts`. Follow `AGENTS.md` for macOS browser execution. These commands do not by themselves run Blender or establish visual approval.

- **Procedural contracts:** unit checks cover animation targets, finite tracks, loop continuity, normalized skin weights, fixed support regions and attached head frames. Legacy GLB roundtrip checks compare sampled deformed vertices and animated node transforms with the live models at three times and save matched PNGs for material review.
- **Native contracts:** `npm run test:dcc` covers declared identities, inputs, delivery profiles, sealed reviews and publication isolation. `npm run test:dcc:saved -- --asset <id>` edits a disposable source through the brief’s declared minimum/interior/maximum control values, verifies fitted moved/fixed probes and save/reload, exports it, then compares native and independent browser samples at five times for every named skinned and rigid mesh. Production source and publication bytes stay hash-pinned. Candidate-dependent cases require their native inputs; a skipped case is not evidence of native execution.
- **Browser delivery:** every still-offered study retains loading, motion and correct download coverage. Shared navigation, save preservation, reduced motion, recording and phone behavior use representative journeys. A workbench-only native release coexists with its procedural gallery study; an accepted gallery-scoped release must use the exact reviewed bytes.
- **Visual review:** inspect desktop, detail, clay/material, back and phone captures of the actual candidate. Finite geometry, sampled motion agreement and phone framing do not establish construction quality or phone performance.

The [Blender package](../../packages/dcc-workbench/README.md#verification-and-provenance) owns native commands and sample limits; the [testing policy](../engineering/testing.md#blender-authoring-and-legacy-compatibility) owns coverage choices. Final native pilot receipts belong to the [pilot record](assets/native-pilots.md).

The 2026-10-06 Nightjar/Phoenix delivery passed **252 unit tests and 11 gallery/shell browser tests**. Final model, video, image and source-hash evidence is under `test-results/sol61-creatures-delivery/`; the [trial record](creature-history.md) separates Sol's four guided rounds per bird from the parent's direct finishing work and records remaining visual limits.
